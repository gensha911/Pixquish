## Instagram image sizes at a glance

| Content type | Dimensions (pixels) | Aspect ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Square post | 1080 × 1080 | 1:1 |
| Portrait post | 1080 × 1350 | 4:5 |
| Landscape post | 1080 × 566 | 1.91:1 |
| Story / Reel | 1080 × 1920 | 9:16 |
| Profile photo | 320 × 320 | 1:1 |
| Reel cover | 1080 × 1920 | 9:16 |
| IGTV cover | 420 × 654 | 1:1.56 |

Instagram compresses anything wider than 1080px, so **always export at exactly 1080px wide** for posts. Larger files get recompressed and can look worse.

## Portrait posts (1080 × 1350)

Portrait posts take up more vertical space in the feed — they're 1.5x taller than square posts. This makes them great for grabbing attention, especially for fashion, product shots, and infographics.

**Tip:** Instagram shows portraits at 4:5 in the feed, so use 1080×1350 for the full image to appear without cropping.

## Stories and Reels (1080 × 1920)

Stories and Reels use the vertical 9:16 format — the same as your phone screen in portrait. This fills the entire screen and is the most immersive format on Instagram.

**How to resize for Stories:**
1. Pick the **Instagram Story** preset (1080×1920)
2. Use **Contain** fit mode if you want the whole image visible with padding
3. Use **Cover** fit mode if you want the image to fill the screen (some cropping)

## Landscape posts (1080 × 566)

Landscape posts are rare on Instagram but useful for wide photos, banners, and cinematic shots. They take up less feed space but look great when used intentionally.

## Profile photo (320 × 320)

Instagram profile photos display at 320×320 but are stored at 1080×1080 internally. Upload a 1080×1080 image for the best quality — Instagram will downscale it for display.

## Best practices for Instagram images

### 1. Always use 1080px width
Instagram compresses images wider than 1080px. If you upload a 4000px photo, Instagram will recompress it — and the result often looks worse than if you'd uploaded 1080px directly.

### 2. Use WebP or JPEG for smaller files
Instagram accepts JPG, PNG, and WebP. WebP produces smaller files with the same quality, which means faster uploads and less data usage. Pixquish can convert your images to WebP before upload.

### 3. Keep file sizes under 1MB
Instagram recompresses anything over ~1MB. If your image is already compressed, it won't get degraded further.

### 4. Use Cover fit mode for full-bleed images
When you resize an image to 1080×1080 but the aspect ratio doesn't match, Cover mode fills the canvas and center-crops the excess. This gives you a full-bleed image with no padding.

### 5. Use Contain mode for logos and text
If your image has important content near the edges (text, logos), use Contain mode — it fits the whole image inside the canvas with padding. Pick a background color that matches your brand.
