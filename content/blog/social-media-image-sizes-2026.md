## Per-platform notes

### Instagram

Instagram crops everything to a square in the feed by default, but you can upload portraits (4:5) and they'll display larger. Stories and Reels are always vertical 9:16. Keep profile photos as JPGs — they're displayed tiny, so PNG's larger file size gives you no benefit.

Use the [Instagram post resizer](/resize/instagram-post) for 1080×1080 squares or the [Instagram story resizer](/resize/instagram-story) for vertical 1080×1920 content. Both are pre-set to exact Instagram dimensions.

### Twitter / X

Twitter compresses any image larger than 1600×900 — uploading a 4000×2250 photo will be auto-downscaled and re-encoded, often with visible artifacts. Upload at the exact recommended size to skip Twitter's lossy re-compression entirely.

Use the [Twitter post resizer](/resize/twitter-post) (1600×900) and the [Twitter header resizer](/resize/twitter-header) (1500×500) for pre-sized outputs that skip Twitter's recompression.

### Facebook

Facebook's link-preview image (1200×630) is what shows when your article link is shared. Upload at this exact size to control how your shared content looks — otherwise Facebook crops randomly. Cover photos display at 820×312 on desktop but 640×360 on mobile, so keep critical content in the center safe zone (avoid the bottom 80px on mobile).

Use the [Facebook post resizer](/resize/facebook-post) for link previews and the [Facebook cover resizer](/resize/facebook-cover) for page covers.

### LinkedIn

LinkedIn banners (1584×396) display very differently on mobile vs desktop — the mobile view crops aggressively to a center horizontal strip. Keep logos and text in the middle 1100×240 area. Profile photos are tiny (under 100×100 in feed) — use a high-contrast JPG, not a detailed PNG.

Use the [LinkedIn banner resizer](/resize/linkedin-banner) to generate the exact 1584×396 banner.

### YouTube

YouTube thumbnails (1280×720) are the single biggest factor in click-through rate. Use bold text, high contrast, and a face if possible. YouTube won't accept images under 640px wide. PNG is fine but JPG is recommended for smaller files.

Use the [YouTube thumbnail resizer](/resize/youtube-thumbnail) to generate 1280×720 thumbnails from any source image.

### Pinterest

Pinterest pins are vertical by design — the 2:3 aspect ratio (1000×1500) performs dramatically better than square images. Taller pins (up to 1:2.1, like 1000×2100) get even more engagement because they take up more screen space in the feed.

Use the [Pinterest pin resizer](/resize/pinterest-pin) to convert any image to the 1000×1500 pin format.

### TikTok

TikTok video covers are 9:16 vertical (1080×1920) — same as Instagram Stories. The Pixquish [Instagram story resizer](/resize/instagram-story) works perfectly for TikTok covers too, since they share the exact same dimensions.

## General best practices for social media images

1. **Always resize before uploading** — every social platform re-compresses images on upload, and they re-compress harder if the image is oversized. Upload at the exact recommended dimensions to skip the platform's recompression.
2. **Use JPG for photos, PNG for graphics with text** — JPGs are smaller and the visual quality difference is invisible at social media display sizes. Use PNG only when your image has thin text or sharp edges.
3. **Keep file sizes under 1 MB** — most platforms cap uploads between 5 and 30 MB, but smaller files upload faster and load quicker on mobile. Use the [Pixquish compress photo](/compress/photo) workflow to crush files before uploading.
4. **Test on mobile** — most social media is consumed on mobile. Crop tightly, use large text, and verify nothing critical sits in the edges where platforms crop differently.
5. **Use Cover fit mode** — when resizing an image of any aspect ratio to a target size, Cover mode preserves the source aspect ratio and crops the overflow. Contain mode letterboxes (adds bars). Stretch mode distorts — never use it for social media.
