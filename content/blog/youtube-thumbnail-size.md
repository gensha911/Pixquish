The correct YouTube thumbnail size is **1280 × 720 pixels** (16:9 aspect ratio). YouTube requires thumbnails to be at least 320×240, but recommends 1280×720 for the best quality on all devices. Here's everything you need to know.

## YouTube thumbnail specs at a glance

| Spec | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Recommended size | 1280 × 720 pixels |
| Minimum size | 320 × 240 pixels |
| Aspect ratio | 16:9 (1.77:1) |
| Max file size | 2 MB |
| Accepted formats | JPG, PNG, GIF, BMP |
| Min width | 640 pixels (for thumbnails to appear in feed) |

### Choosing a fit mode

YouTube thumbnails must be exactly 1280×720. If your image has a different aspect ratio, you have three options:

**Cover** (recommended for most photos)
- Fills the entire 1280×720 canvas
- Crops the excess from the edges
- Best for photos where the subject is centered

**Contain**
- Fits the whole image inside 1280×720
- Adds black bars (or any color you choose) to fill the empty space
- Best when you need to show the entire image, like a product shot or logo

**Stretch**
- Distorts the image to fit 1280×720 exactly
- Not recommended — makes things look weird
- Pixquish shows a distortion warning if you pick this

## Best practices for YouTube thumbnails

### 1. Use high contrast
Thumbnails appear small in search results and the sidebar. High contrast between your subject and background makes them pop. Dark subject on light background, or vice versa.

### 2. Add text — but make it readable
Text overlays increase click-through rates significantly, but the text must be readable at 320×180 (the size thumbnails appear in the sidebar). Use:
- 4–6 words maximum
- Bold, thick fonts
- High contrast colors (white text on dark background with outline)
- Large font size — at least 80px at 1280×720 dimensions

### 3. Show faces with emotion
Thumbnails with expressive faces get more clicks. The face should take up 30–50% of the thumbnail. If you have multiple people, have them look at each other or at the camera.

### 4. Use the rule of thirds
Don't center your subject perfectly — place it at the intersection of thirds (left third or right third). This creates visual interest and leaves space for text on the other side.

### 5. Keep it under 2 MB
YouTube limits thumbnails to 2 MB. If your thumbnail is larger, compress it. Pixquish can compress your final thumbnail to under 2 MB with the target file size feature (set to 2 MB or 1.5 MB to be safe).

### 6. Test at small sizes
Before uploading, view your thumbnail at 320×180. If you can't read the text or see the subject clearly, it won't work in YouTube's sidebar. Make adjustments.

## Common thumbnail mistakes

### Wrong dimensions
YouTube will reject or stretch your thumbnail if it's not 16:9. Always resize to exactly 1280×720 before uploading. Use the YouTube Thumbnail preset in Pixquish to guarantee correct dimensions.

### Text too small
Text that looks great at 1280×720 might be unreadable at 320×180. Test at small sizes before uploading. If you can't read it, increase font size.

### Subject too far away
A full-body shot at 1280×720 will have a tiny subject when the thumbnail is shown at 320×180. Zoom in on the subject — faces should be at least 30% of the frame.

### Too many elements
A cluttered thumbnail confuses viewers. Stick to one subject, one piece of text, one background element. Simplicity wins.

### Low contrast
A gray subject on a gray background disappears in the sidebar. Make sure your subject stands out from the background.

## Thumbnail dimensions for different YouTube placements

YouTube shows your thumbnail at different sizes depending on where it appears:

| Placement | Display size |
|---|---|
| YouTube homepage (desktop) | ~360 × 202 |
| YouTube search results | ~246 × 138 (or larger on some layouts) |
| Sidebar suggested videos | ~168 × 94 |
| Mobile feed | ~340 × 191 |
| Watch page (main video preview) | 1280 × 720 (full size) |
| Channel page | ~246 × 138 |

Design your thumbnail to look good at the smallest size (~168 × 94) — if it works there, it'll work everywhere.

## File format for YouTube thumbnails

YouTube accepts JPG, PNG, GIF, and BMP. Here's what to use:

- **JPG:** Best for photos. Small file size, universal support.
- **PNG:** Best for graphics with text. Lossless, supports transparency (though YouTube will add a background). Larger file size.
- **GIF:** Animated thumbnails are possible but distracting. Avoid.
- **BMP:** No reason to use this in 2026.

**Recommendation:** Use JPG for photo-based thumbnails, PNG if you have lots of text and need maximum sharpness.
